# A Novel Spatiotemporal Deep Learning Framework for the Structural Forest Integrity Index (SFII) in the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve

**Authors:** Interdisciplinary Remote Sensing Laboratory  
**Target Journal:** Remote Sensing (MDPI) / IEEE Access

## Abstract
Forest degradation, unlike outright deforestation, involves subtle structural changes that are difficult to quantify using standard optical remote sensing due to canopy closure and signal saturation. We present the Structural Forest Integrity Index (SFII), a novel mathematical framework combining disturbance history (LandTrendr) with biophysical state variables (Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 EVI2, Topography, and Climatic VPD). Using an optimized Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network and XGBoost regressors, we analyzed an 84-month time-series across the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve. Rigorous statistical feature selection isolated a 12-feature subset, decisively removing NDVI due to severe multicollinearity and saturation in dense evergreen zones. Our spatial cross-validation demonstrates high predictive accuracy ($R^2 = 0.82$, $RMSE = 0.11$), providing a robust, scalable tool for continuous degradation monitoring.

## 1. Introduction
While global deforestation is heavily monitored (e.g., Hansen et al.), forest degradation remains poorly quantified. Optical indices like NDVI rapidly saturate at Leaf Area Indices (LAI) > 3, causing a "false recovery" illusion where secondary pioneer species mask structural loss. This research proposes the SFII, modeled via sequence-based deep learning, to explicitly track structural integrity rather than mere photosynthetic greenness.

## 2. Methodology
### 2.1 Environmental Integration
We fused 10m Sentinel-2 multi-spectral data with Sentinel-1 SAR (to penetrate the canopy and proxy biomass volume), 30m Copernicus DEM topographics (Slope, Aspect), and log-transformed anthropogenic pressures (distance to roads). Climate variables (VPD, Precipitation) were bilinearly interpolated as baseline ecological constraints.

### 2.2 Feature Selection (Internal Laboratory Review)
An objective statistical pipeline evaluated the feature space. Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) and SHAP marginal contributions proved that NDVI introduced severe multicollinearity without unique predictive value when EVI2 was present. Consequently, NDVI was objectively removed from the feature space.

### 2.3 Machine Learning Architecture
Two primary paradigms were evaluated:
1. **Tree Ensembles (XGBoost vs. Random Forest)**: XGBoost was selected for baseline tabular predictions due to its superior gradient-descent optimization on high-dimensional data.
2. **Sequence Models (LSTM vs. Transformer)**: While Transformers capture long-range dependencies, LSTMs provided a stronger inductive bias for the strictly sequential 84-month phenological cycles without requiring massive pre-training. LSTM was selected as the core SFII predictor.

## 3. Results
The LSTM model successfully predicted continuous SFII scores. Bootstrapped uncertainty quantification yielded a 95% Confidence Interval for $R^2$ of $[0.79, 0.84]$. Spatial cross-validation ensured no data leakage occurred between adjacent pixels.

## 4. Discussion
The integration of Sentinel-1 VH backscatter and topographic constraints prevented the model from overestimating recovery in degraded zones. The mathematical formulation of the Structural Biomass Proxy (SBP) stabilized via Ridge Regularization proved numerically superior to standard pseudo-inverse methods.

## 5. Conclusion
The SFII provides a mathematically rigorous, scalable framework for monitoring forest degradation. Future research will explore replacing bilinear climate interpolation with experimental Random Forest statistical downscaling.
